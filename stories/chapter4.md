## Chapter 4 — Polar Return

Curiosity dragged Atlas north again, chasing rumors of icebergs that sing. He boarded an icebreaker in Reykjavik, traded tricks for leftover lamb, and learned the creak-language of shifting floes.

Under the aurora’s second curtain, he discovered a research husky named Freyja who raced him across blue-white runways. Their breath made twin clouds; their shadows stitched across the snow like hurried signatures.

When a blizzard stalled the ship, Atlas smelled trouble first—diesel sharp, wind wrong. He herded the crew below deck just before a slab of ice calved and rolled, cracking the hull but sparing lives. They patched with steel and gratitude; someone painted his name on the bow in red lead.

With the thaw, he traced his way back toward Montreal, the painted name fading in salt spray. The world had given him charms and scars, but also a map of friendships. He carried it in his muscles and memories, ready to curl beside Lenny again, heartbeat synced to home.
