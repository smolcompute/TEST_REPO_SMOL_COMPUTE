## Chapter 3 — Desert Lines

Atlas rode a freight train through the American Southwest, nose pressed to the slats as mesas marched past in rusted procession. At night, coyotes called him cousin; he answered with silence and a raised tail—respectful, not submissive.

In Morocco, the Sahara hit like an oven door. A caravan guide wrapped a blue scarf around Atlas’s neck, wetting it from a goatskin. They crossed dunes by starlight, the dog reading camel footprints the way others read newsprint. He learned water is a promise, not a luxury.

At an oasis near Timbuktu, Atlas met a Tuareg poet who carved verses into leather. Together they raced dust devils and watched nomad kids kite beetle-bright drones. The poet etched a line for Atlas: “Bravery is remembering the way home after the wind erases it.”
